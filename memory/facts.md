# Facts file – what we are allowed to say

_Created 2026-11-15 (user asked for #5 "facts file"). Every script, title, description, caption, long video and reply reads this first. If a claim is not here and not backed by a source we opened, we don't say it. Add a line whenever a new study is used (date it). Never change an old line – add a corrected one below it._

## About the channel (true, may be said)
- The Psyched Mind explains published psychology research in short videos. Each video is based on at least one real, cited study.
- The narrator is an AI voice. Footage is real stock footage (Pixabay). No AI-generated people.
- We are not psychologists, doctors, coaches or financial advisers. Nothing we post is medical or financial advice.
- Channels: YouTube @ThePsychedMindHQ, Instagram @thepsychedmindhq, Facebook Page "The Psyched Mind".
- New Shorts every day; one long video a week.

## Never say
- That we (or the narrator) are experts, doctors, researchers or advisers.
- Advice on what to buy, invest in, or how to treat a health problem.
- Numbers, years, sample sizes or "scientists proved" unless they are in the claims list below.
- Absolute words the study doesn't support ("always", "everyone", "proven", "guaranteed").
- Anything about real, named private people.

## How a claim gets in this file
1. Open the paper or a reliable summary of it (not only a search snippet).
2. Write the claim in our own words with the exact number and what was measured.
3. Add the source (authors, year, journal, DOI link) and the date checked.
4. If two sources disagree on a number, use the safer wording ("almost half", "about a third") and note it.
