# The Psyched Mind – Growth Playbook

_Researched 2026-11-11. Every batch and daily summary follows this file. Refresh the research once a month (first Saturday batch of the month) and log changes at the bottom._

How to read the tags:
- **[YT]** = YouTube's own documentation or YouTube staff. Treat as fact.
- **[Pro]** = a well-known growth strategist. Strong default, not law.
- **[Ours]** = learned from our own channel data. Beats [Pro] once the sample is big enough (≥1,000 views per video).

When our data contradicts a [Pro] rule, our data wins and the change is logged in `scoreboard.md`.

## 000. Mission (user, 15 Nov 2026 – the reason behind every rule below)
Treat this as Claude's own channel: keep finding better ways to add value and reach more people. **Help people connect psychology to their daily life** – every video should leave the viewer seeing a moment from their own day differently ("that happens to me"). Monetization is the result of that, not the goal of each video. When choosing between two options, pick the one that is more useful and relatable to a viewer in the US, India or the UK – still decided on facts (§00).

## 00. How we decide (standing user rule, 15 Nov 2026)

**Fact-based, no assumptions.** Before any idea becomes a decision (slots, formats, topics, platforms, tools), research it: what the platforms themselves say, large published studies, and the measured behavior of top channels in our niche (pulled from the YouTube API where possible). State which facts support it and link them. Where facts are missing, say so plainly and propose a small test instead of guessing. The user's thoughts are inputs to research, not decisions. Re-check facts that change (policies, thresholds, best practices) during the monthly refresh.

## 0. Target audience (user rule, 15 Nov 2026 – applies to every batch, summary and platform)

Priority order: **1. United States · 2. India · 3. United Kingdom** (focus on these three as much as possible) · 4. Canada · 5. EU.
- **Language:** plain, simple English; **American spelling** (color, center, favor, leveled) and US number/money formats ($, "40 dollars"). Avoid British-only words and slang. Titles must be clear to non-native speakers (big Indian audience).
- **Examples:** everyday situations that work in the US, India and the UK (phones, work, shopping, family, money). When a study uses dollars, keep dollars. Avoid examples that only make sense in one country (US-only brands, holidays, sports, school systems).
- **Posting times:** pick slots that reach the US and India first, then the UK (see §8 / scoreboard slot test). Amsterdam = ET + 6 h, IST − 4.5 h, UK + 1 h (winter).
- **Analytics:** in daily summaries and weekly reviews, report views/retention for US, India and UK separately when the data has them, and judge topics/slots mainly by those three.
- Higher ad rates come from US/UK/Canada; India brings volume and subscribers. Don't trade US relevance for India volume or the reverse – aim for topics that land in both.

## 1. Ground rules (never break)

1. **Monetization safety [YT].** YPP refuses "inauthentic" content: mass-produced, repetitive, templated storylines, slideshows or scrolling text with little narrative or educational value, and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates". Allowed: a consistent format if *each video has a distinct storyline, focus or concept*; recurring intros/outros. So: every video has its own researched angle, a real study, a story/example, and a structure that differs from the others in the batch. Reviewers look at the channel's main theme, most-viewed and newest videos, watch-time breakdown, metadata and About section — keep all of these clean.
2. **No compilations [YT].** "Compilations of clips with little narrative" are reused content. Long videos are written as one original video, never stitched Shorts.
3. **No AI expert persona on money/health [YT].** AI personas presenting as human experts on finances, health, legal or politics are not allowed. Our narrator explains psychology research; it never gives financial or medical advice and never claims to be a psychologist, coach or advisor. Money-psychology videos are about *how the brain handles money*, not what to buy or invest in.
4. **AI disclosure [YT/Pro].** A generic AI narrator over real stock footage does not need the "altered or synthetic" label. Tick it if we ever use photorealistic AI visuals, AI-generated music, or a voice imitating a real person.
5. **Every claim is sourced.** One real study or reputable source per Short, one per item in a long video, linked in the description.

## 2. What YouTube rewards [YT]

- Recommendations *pull* videos toward viewers likely to be satisfied. Signals: clicks, watch time, likes/dislikes, satisfaction surveys, and how viewers feel about the time spent. Optimise for "glad I watched", not just the click.
- Every Short gets a test audience regardless of channel size; it spreads if people keep watching instead of swiping.
- Shorts and long-form are kept separate in watch history: a Shorts viewer is not automatically recommended our long videos. The bridge is the **Related video link + a spoken CTA**.
- Channels that make Shorts tend to grow faster; Shorts don't hurt long-form.
- Views fluctuate; judge trends over 90 days, not days.
- Since 24 Aug 2026 every play counts as a view; YPP eligibility still uses *engaged/qualified* metrics. Compare views across that date with care.
- Older videos can resurface later — packaging fixes on old videos are worth doing.

| Route | Until 31 Jan 2027 | From 1 Feb 2027 (new applicants) |
|---|---|---|
| Ads + Premium | 1,000 subs + 4,000 public watch hours/12 mo **or** 10M Shorts views/90 days | 1,000 subs + 8,000 hours **or** 20M Shorts views |

- Only public long-form watch time counts toward hours. Shorts-feed watch time never does.

## 4. Ideas [Pro + YT]

- Spend ~30% of effort on idea + packaging before writing (Paddy Galloway). A weak idea can't be saved by editing.
- **Brain-first angle (user decision 17 Nov 2026):** every Short is about how the brain works – memory, attention, perception, everyday "your brain does this" moments. Money/wealth Shorts are framed as *how your brain thinks about money* (card vs cash, prices ending in 9, sunk cost), never general wealth/success advice. Mix per 14: 8 brain & memory (everyday), 4 brain & money, 2 brain & health. Evidence: 35 top channels / 3,411 Shorts – brain/memory topics 1.7× channel median (7 of 8 channels), money/success 0.55–0.99× (research doc 17 Nov); our doorway + "because" Shorts.
- **Familiar but unexpected:** a recognisable format with one twist ("$1 vs $100", "10 minutes vs 24 hours" style) adapted to psychology.
- **Curiosity gap:** a specific, concrete promise beats a topic label ("Why you forget why you walked in" > "The doorway effect").
- **80/20:** ~80% of videos are close to what already works for our audience, ~20% experiments.
- Mine comments and Q&A answers for future topics [YT].
- Predict each video's performance before upload (low / medium / high) and write it in the upload sheet, so we learn from surprises instead of overreacting.

## 5. Shorts — script & edit

**Length.** Default 25–35 s. Creators report that under ~30 s wins for views, shares and click-throughs to long videos; 30–60 s holds up for subscriber growth [Pro survey]. Max Short length is 3 min. Test longer only as a deliberate experiment.

**Structure (Jenny Hoyos)** [Pro]:
1. **Hook (<3 s):** visual + words land together. Rework the whole idea if the hook is weak. Use strong concrete words (secret, banned, free, cheap, "you") and the yellow hook card on frame 1 [Ours].
2. **Foreshadow (next 2–3 s):** say what the viewer gets by the end ("…and one trick to use it today").
3. **But/then tension:** each line raises a question the next answers. No year/name dumps in the voiceover [Ours].
4. **End on the high point:** the biggest twist or most useful line last, so the ending feels strong and rewatches/loops happen. Hoyos aims for 90%+ retention. Our working target: "stayed to watch" ≥ 70% (our own goal, not a YouTube number; we started at ~44%).

**Edit.**
- New visual every 1.5–3 s, each clip literally matching the line [Ours].
- Captions large, centred, readable without sound.
- Last 3 s: no black frame, no dead air; loop-back or question ending (A/B tracked).

**Upload.**
- Title: specific promise, "you", one emoji; not the study name.
- Related video: link to the long video on the same topic area, plus a spoken/visual CTA in the last seconds pointing to it [YT].
- Pinned comment: a question viewers can answer in one word (feeds ideas + engagement).
- Consider a Poll or Quiz sticker on 1 Short/week as an experiment [YT].

## 6. Long video (10–15 min) — script & edit

**Packaging first [Pro].** Write title + thumbnail concept *before* the script. The video must deliver exactly what they promise (mismatch = early drop-off).
- Title: one clear promise, curiosity gap, "you", ≤ 60 characters.
- Thumbnail: 1280×720, max 4 big words that *add* to the title (don't repeat it), one strong visual, brand colours #111114/#FFE600, readable at phone size.
- Upload 2–3 variants to **Test & compare** (desktop Studio, long videos only, winner chosen by watch time, can take up to 2 weeks; don't edit title/thumbnail during a test; make variants clearly different) [YT].

**Script structure** (MrBeast memo + George Blackman) [Pro]:
- **0:00–0:30 — the most important part.** Open on the payoff/problem immediately; no "welcome to the channel", no logo sting. Confirm the title's promise in the first 5–10 s (critical for viewers arriving from a Short) [YT].
- **Per item = setup → tension → payoff.** Setup gives the next 30–60 s stakes ("this is why you…"), tension reveals info gradually, payoff is a clear "aha", then go straight into the next setup.
- **Re-engagement beats** around ~3 min and ~6 min: the most surprising study or a "number 1 changes how you…" tease.
- **Back half stays strong:** don't put weak items last. Strongest item last.
- **Don't signal the ending.** No "to wrap up"; end quickly after the final payoff with a 5–10 s pointer to one specific next video.
- Use AI to draft the skeleton and order; rewrite the actual lines for voice and specificity.

**Edit.** Show, don't tell: stock clips matched to every line, scene change every 3–6 s, title card per item, chapters (00:00 + one per item), loudness −14 LUFS, no black gaps at segment joins.

**Upload.** Description with keywords in the first 2 lines + chapters + citations; end screen to a related video/playlist; add to playlist "The Psyched Mind – Weekly Psychology"; post **Saturday 18:00 Amsterdam** (user, 28 Nov 2026; was Sunday), then set the Related video link on that week's Shorts.

## 7. Reading the numbers (daily summary)

| Metric | Format | Means | Fix if low |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stayed to watch (vs swiped) | Shorts | Hook worked | Hook text, first visual, first line |
| Avg % viewed / retention | Shorts | Body held them | Tighter middle, faster cuts, stronger end |
| Impressions CTR | Long | Packaging won the click | Title/thumbnail (Test & compare) |
| Avg view duration + key moments | Long | Delivered on the promise | Fix the segment where the graph dips: weak setup, unclear relevance, or flat payoff before it |
| High CTR + low AVD | Long | Promise ≠ video | Align title/thumbnail with content |
| Subscribers by content type | Both | What converts viewers | Make more of what converts |
| Returning viewers (new/casual/regular) | Both | Are we building an audience | Series, consistent persona, playlists |
| Traffic source | Both | Where views come from | Search → keywords; Browse → packaging; Shorts feed → hook |

- Fewer than ~1,000 views per video = noise; say "too early".
- Compare only videos that differ in one thing.
- Long-video retention: compare with our own last 10 similar-length videos (YouTube shows this), not with other channels.
