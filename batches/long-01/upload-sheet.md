## Description
```
Why do you spend more with a card, overpay after seeing a "was" price, or keep stuff you never use? Your brain handles money in predictable – and expensive – ways. Here are 7 real psychology studies that show how (and the one way money reliably makes people happier).

00:00 Your brain is costing you money
00:25 1. The pain of paying (cash vs card)
01:22 2. The anchor price
02:13 3. The decoy
03:06 4. Mental accounting
04:09 5. Why you overvalue what you own
05:07 6. Present bias (and the savings trick)
06:14 7. Money and happiness

Studies:
1. Prelec & Simester (2001), Marketing Letters – https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008196717017
2. Northcraft & Neale (1987), Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes – https://doi.org/10.1016/0749-5978(87)90046-X
3. Huber, Payne & Puto (1982), Journal of Consumer Research – https://doi.org/10.1086/208899 (popcorn prices are an illustration)
4. Tversky & Kahneman (1981), Science – https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7455683
5. Kahneman, Knetsch & Thaler (1990), Journal of Political Economy – https://doi.org/10.1086/261737
6. Thaler & Benartzi (2004), Journal of Political Economy – https://doi.org/10.1086/380085 (the $100 vs $110 question is an illustration)
7. Dunn, Aknin & Norton (2008), Science – https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1150952

This video explains psychology research about how the brain handles money. It is not financial advice.
```
