## Descriptions

**Why your brain remembers the ending most 🧠**
Why do you remember the worst part of an experience and how it ended, but forget the rest? Psychologists call it the peak-end rule.
In one study, 69% of people chose to repeat a LONGER cold-water trial, because it ended slightly less painfully.
Source: Kahneman, Fredrickson, Schreiber & Redelmeier (1993), Psychological Science – https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1993.tb00589.x
Science-backed psychology · Sources in description · Not medical advice.

**Why you spend more with a card than cash 💳**
Why do you spend more money when you pay by card? Psychologists call it the "pain of paying": handing over cash feels like a loss, tapping a card doesn't.
In a famous auction for sold-out basketball tickets, students told to pay by credit card bid more than twice as much as those paying cash.
Source: Prelec & Simester (2001), Marketing Letters – https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008196717017
Science-backed psychology about how the brain handles money · Not financial advice.
#psychology #money #spendinghabits #moneypsychology #shorts
Pinned comment: Cash or card – which makes YOU spend more?

**Can't sleep? Write this down before bed 🌙**
Can't sleep because your mind keeps racing about tomorrow's tasks? Unfinished tasks keep pulling your attention back – but writing a specific plan for them switches that off, even before the task is done.
In a sleep-lab study, people who wrote a to-do list before bed fell asleep faster than people who wrote about tasks they'd already finished.
Sources: Masicampo & Baumeister (2011), JPSP – https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024192 · Scullin et al. (2018), Journal of Experimental Psychology: General – https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000374
Science-backed psychology · Sources in description · Not medical advice.
#psychology #cantsleep #overthinking #sleeptips #shorts

**Why you forget why you walked into a room 🚪**
Ever walk into a room and forget why you went in? It's not your memory failing – it's the "doorway effect".
Walking through a doorway makes your brain close one "event" and start a new one, so the old plan gets filed away.
Source: Radvansky, Krawietz & Tamplin (2011), Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology – https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2011.571267
Science-backed psychology · Sources in description · Not medical advice.
#psychology #memory #doorwayeffect #brain #shorts

**Why making mistakes makes people like you more ☕**
Why do small mistakes make people like you more? It's called the pratfall effect – but it only works if people already see you as competent.
Source: Aronson, Willerman & Floyd (1966), Psychonomic Science.
Science-backed psychology · Sources in description · Not medical advice.
#psychology #confidence #pratfalleffect #socialskills #shorts

**Everyone noticed your mistake? Science says no 😳**
Worried everyone noticed your embarrassing moment? They mostly didn't – it's called the spotlight effect.
Students made to wear an embarrassing T-shirt guessed half the room noticed; in reality only about a quarter did.
Source: Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000), JPSP – https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.211
Science-backed psychology · Sources in description · Not medical advice.
#psychology #spotlighteffect #socialanxiety #confidence #shorts

**People like you more than you think 🧠**
Do you replay conversations and think people didn't like you? Research says they liked you more than you think. It's called the liking gap.
After chatting with strangers, almost everyone underestimated how much the other person liked them – and among new college roommates the gap lasted for months.
Source: Boothby, Cooney, Sandstrom & Clark (2018), Psychological Science – https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618783714
Science-backed psychology · Sources in description · Not medical advice.
#psychology #overthinking #socialanxiety #likinggap #shorts
