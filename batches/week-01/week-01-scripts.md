## 1. Spotlight effect
**Video Subject:** Why nobody noticed your embarrassing moment
**Video Script:**
You think everyone noticed your embarrassing moment. They didn't. In a Cornell study, students had to walk into a room wearing an embarrassing Barry Manilow T-shirt. They guessed about half the room noticed. In reality, fewer than a quarter did. It's called the spotlight effect. You're the main character in your own head, so you assume you're the main character in everyone else's too. But they're busy worrying about their own spotlight. What moment do you still cringe about?
**Video Keywords:** embarrassed person, crowded room, stage spotlight, people walking street, students classroom, person blushing
**YouTube title:** Everyone noticed your mistake? Science says no 😳
**Description:** You're far less noticed than you think. Gilovich et al. (2000) called it the spotlight effect. #psychology #psychologyfacts #spotlighteffect #humanbehavior #shorts
**Fact check:** Shirt-wearers estimated ~50% noticed; fewer than 25% could identify it. https://psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/the-social-self/200906/did-everyone-see-me-do-that

## 2. Liking gap
**Video Subject:** People like you more than you think
**Video Script:**
That conversation you keep replaying? They probably liked you more than you think. Researchers had strangers chat, then asked each person two questions. How much do you like them? And how much do they like you? Almost everyone underestimated how much they were liked. It's called the liking gap. While you're judging your own awkward pauses, they're just enjoying the conversation. Among new college roommates, the gap lasted for months. Who do you need to stop overthinking about?
**Video Keywords:** two people talking coffee, friends conversation, person thinking at night, smiling conversation, overthinking, college students
**YouTube title:** They liked you more than you think 🧠
**Description:** The "liking gap": after conversations we underestimate how much others like us (Boothby et al., 2018). #psychology #psychologyfacts #overthinking #socialanxiety #shorts
**Fact check:** Five studies; dorm-mates underestimated liking from September until May. https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/liking-gap

## 3. Doorway effect
**Video Subject:** Why you forget things when you walk into a room
**Video Script:**
Walked into a room and forgot why you came? You're not losing your memory. Your brain is doing housekeeping. Researchers at Notre Dame found people forgot more after walking through a doorway than after walking the same distance inside one room. A doorway works like a scene change in a movie. Your brain files away the last scene to make room for the new one, and your reason for walking in gets filed with it. What's the weirdest thing you've forgotten in a doorway?
**Video Keywords:** walking through door, opening door, confused person, hallway house, person forgetting, brain
**YouTube title:** Why doorways make you forget 🚪
**Description:** It's called the doorway effect (Radvansky et al., 2011). Your brain closes one "event" and starts another. #psychology #psychologyfacts #memory #brain #shorts
**Fact check:** Recall was slower and less accurate after passing a doorway vs. the same distance in one room. https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/why-walking-through-doorway-makes-you-forget/

## 4. Pratfall effect
**Video Subject:** Why mistakes can make people like you more
**Video Script:**
Think a mistake makes people respect you less? Not always. In a 1966 experiment, students listened to a quiz contestant who got almost every answer right. Then, in one version, he spilled coffee all over himself. Listeners liked him more after the spill. It's called the pratfall effect. A small blunder makes a competent person feel human. But there's a catch. When the contestant was mediocre, the same spill made people like him less. So be good first. Then you're allowed to be clumsy.
**Video Keywords:** spilled coffee, person laughing at mistake, quiz show, office meeting, clumsy person, applause
**YouTube title:** Why mistakes make people like you more ☕
**Description:** The pratfall effect (Aronson, 1966). It only works if you're already competent. #psychology #psychologyfacts #confidence #humanbehavior #shorts
**Fact check:** Contestant answered 92% correctly; spill increased liking. With 30% correct, spill decreased liking. https://weforum.org/agenda/2018/02/why-admitting-a-weakness-makes-people-like-you-more

## 5. Ben Franklin effect
**Video Subject:** Ask for a favor to make someone like you
**Video Script:**
Want someone to like you? Don't do them a favor. Ask them for one. Benjamin Franklin had a rival who disliked him, so he asked to borrow a rare book from him. He returned it with a thank-you note, and the man became a friend for life. Psychologists later tested it. People who were personally asked for a favor ended up liking the person more. Your brain thinks, I helped them, so I must like them. Who would you try this on?
**Video Keywords:** lending a book, handshake, old library books, two people smiling, friendship, vintage desk
**YouTube title:** The favor trick that makes people like you 📖
**Description:** The Ben Franklin effect: helping someone makes us like them more (Jecker & Landy, 1969). #psychology #psychologyfacts #benfranklin #socialskills #shorts
**Fact check:** Students asked directly by the researcher to return prize money liked him most. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Franklin_effect

## 6. IKEA effect
**Video Subject:** Why you love things you built yourself
**Video Script:**
Think you love your furniture because it's good? It might be because you built it. In one study, people assembled simple IKEA storage boxes, then bid on them. The builders were willing to pay sixty-three percent more for their own box than other people paid for the exact same box, already assembled. It's called the IKEA effect. Effort makes us overvalue what we make. That's why your wobbly shelf feels priceless. What's something you made that you'd never sell?
**Video Keywords:** assembling furniture, flat pack furniture, diy woodworking, person building shelf, cardboard box, proud person
**YouTube title:** Why your wobbly shelf feels priceless 🔨
**Description:** The IKEA effect (Norton, Mochon & Ariely, 2012): builders paid 63% more. #psychology #psychologyfacts #ikeaeffect #behavioraleconomics #shorts
**Fact check:** Builders bid $0.78 vs. $0.48 for pre-assembled (63% premium). https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstream/handle/1/12136084/norton%20mochon%20ariely%20third%20round[1].pdf

## 7. Mere exposure effect
**Video Subject:** Why you like things you see often
**Video Script:**
You think you like that song because it's good. Often, you've just heard it a lot. In 1968, psychologist Robert Zajonc showed people made-up words, strange symbols and faces. Some once, some up to twenty-five times. The more often people saw something, the more they liked it, even though it meant nothing to them. It's called the mere exposure effect. It's why ads repeat and songs grow on you. What's something you hated at first and now love?
**Video Keywords:** person listening headphones, city billboard, music concert, repeating pattern, smiling face, scrolling phone
**YouTube title:** You don't like it. You've just seen it a lot 🎧
**Description:** The mere exposure effect (Zajonc, 1968): familiarity breeds liking. #psychology #psychologyfacts #mereexposure #marketing #shorts
**Fact check:** Liking rose with exposure frequency (0–25 times) for nonsense words, ideographs and faces. https://www.simplypsychology.org/mere-exposure-effect.html
