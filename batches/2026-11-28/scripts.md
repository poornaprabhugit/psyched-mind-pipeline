## Q01 · Mon 30 Nov 12:00 · A (animated) · predicted high
**Hook card:** Found a wallet with cash. Return it?
**Title:** Would you return a lost wallet with money in it? 👛
**Script:**
You're more likely to return a wallet with cash in it. Researchers turned in over seventeen thousand "lost" wallets in forty countries. Empty ones came back forty percent of the time. With money inside? Fifty-one percent. Most people guessed the opposite. Because ↻
**Source:** Cohn, Maréchal, Tannenbaum & Zünd (2019), Science – https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aau8712 (17,303 wallets, 355 cities, 40 countries incl. India; 40% no money vs 51% with $13.45; laypeople and economists predicted the reverse)
**Caption note:** Works in any country: India was one of the 40.

## Q02 · Mon 30 Nov 16:00 · K (stock) · predicted medium
**Hook card:** Snack for next week: fruit or chocolate?
**Title:** Why you choose healthy food for later, but not now 🍎
**Script:**
You're more likely to pick fruit for later, and chocolate for now. Two hundred workers chose a snack they'd get a week later. Many chose an apple or a banana. A week later, they could choose again, right then. Far more took chips or a chocolate bar. Future you eats well. Right-now you doesn't. That's why ↻
**Source:** Read & van Leeuwen (1998), Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes – https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1998.2803 (N=200 employees of Amsterdam firms; healthy = apples, bananas; unhealthy = crisps/chips, coated nuts, Mars, Snickers; snack chosen a week ahead, then chosen again at the moment of eating; "far more unhealthy snacks for immediate choice than for advance choice". No overall % in the voiceover: the paper gives it only in a figure.)

## Q03 · Tue 1 Dec 12:00 · K (stock) · predicted medium
**Hook card:** They keep $90, you get $10. Deal?
**Title:** Why you'd turn down free money 💵
**Script:**
You'd probably say no to free money if the split feels unfair. In a classic game, one person gets some money and offers you a share. Say yes, you both get paid. Say no, nobody gets anything. On average, people offer about forty percent. Low offers often get turned down. Fair beats free. So ↻
**Source:** Güth, Schmittberger & Schwarze (1982), Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization 3(4) (the original game); Oosterbeek, Sloof & van de Kuilen (2004), Experimental Economics – https://doi.org/10.1023/B:EXEC.0000026978.14316.74 (meta-analysis of 37 papers: mean offer 40.4%, mean rejection 16.2%). Low offers (≤20%) rejected ~60% in Xiao & Houser (2005), cited in Cooper & Kagel's survey. "Often" is used in place of a number.

**Title:** Why everything takes longer than you planned ⏳
**Script:**
Your guess for when you'll finish is probably too early. Students guessed when they'd finish their big final paper. Average guess: thirty-four days. Real time: fifty-six. Fewer than one in three finished on time. About half missed even their worst-case guess. So ↻

**Script:**
You'll say yes far more often when yes is already checked. In an online study, people chose whether to be organ donors. When they had to check a box to join, far fewer joined. When they were in unless they unchecked it, about twice as many stayed in. The default chose for them. That's why ↻
**Source:** Johnson & Goldstein (2003), Science – https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1091721; numbers checked in Johnson & Goldstein (2004), Transplantation 78 (N=161; opt-out ~twice opt-in; neutral 79%). Secondary (AMA Journal of Ethics 2005): opt-in 42%, opt-out 82%. The exact % are not in the voiceover because the primary text was not opened.
